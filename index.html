About

Research with a reliability-first perspective.

My work connects embodied artificial intelligence, geometric deep learning, safety-aware decision systems, and university teaching.

I am a computer science researcher and university educator with research experience spanning embodied AI, vision-language-action models, geometric deep learning, protein–ligand pose prediction, explainable federated learning, and reliable autonomous systems.

My doctoral research at Auckland University of Technology focuses on reliability-centred decision-making for embodied agents. The central question is not only whether an AI model can propose a plausible action, but whether the surrounding system can ground that action in physical constraints, predict its feasibility, recall causally relevant failures, and arbitrate safety before execution.

Before beginning my doctoral research, I completed a thesis-based M.Sc. in Computer Science and Engineering at Bangladesh University of Engineering and Technology. My master's work developed transformation-invariant and geometric deep-learning methods for protein–ligand pose correction and iterative refinement.

Alongside research, I have more than five years of university teaching experience and have contributed to outcome-based education, curriculum development, academic quality assurance, student mentoring, research preparation, and programming activities.

Research

Toward cognitive architectures for reliable autonomy.

I study how autonomous agents can make dependable decisions across long horizons, especially when actions must remain physically feasible, causally informed, and safe.

Toward Cognitive Architectures for Reliable Long-Horizon Planning in LLM-Based Embodied Agents

My doctoral work proposes a Cognitive Reliability Loop: a modular decision layer combining constraint-aware planning, feasibility prediction, causal memory, and safety arbitration around a vision-language-action policy. The research is designed for initial evaluation using OpenVLA and the LIBERO simulation benchmark, with staged progression toward Unitree G1 humanoid deployment.
